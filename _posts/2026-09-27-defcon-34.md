I had heard about DEFCON for the first time in the spring of 2026. At the time, I was just wrapping up my four years at Western New England University, preparing for graduation and what might come after. Graduating with a degree in computer science and working closely with the IT department, I was surprised to realize I had never heard of the largest cybersecurity/hacker event in the world. I was introduced to the conference by my father, as I had vocalized to him earlier in the year that I might start looking at IT/cybersecurity jobs and training after school. He had learned about DEFCON from a coworker and believed it would be a good place to network and learn about specific aspects of the field. I was very eager to see what the global communities of the trade had in store for us, so I started preparing for our trip.

The conference was set to take place the first week of August in Las Vegas. Well before any bags were packed or plans were made, I started by researching the conference itself. I pored over the conference's website, as well as reviews and videos about what had been offered in previous years. Through all this information, I put together a rough picture of what the event was and what it meant for me. The first realization I made was the scale of the event. Attendance this year was expected to be around 30,000, which was an intimidatingly large number. Knowing this, I immediately started to think negatively. There were 30,000 people attending this event, most of whom had experience within this field. I felt completely out of my element and wondered if everything at the conference would go over my head. This led to another realization. Out of 30,000 attendees, it was impractical to assume that everyone was beyond entry level. There was no way that, out of that massive pool, I would be the only beginner. This eased my mind, and immediately the excitement for the event returned. With the realization that there would be other beginners experiencing DEFCON for the first time as well, I figured that there might be events or stations dedicated to newcomers. I began to research various groups that were catered to beginners and found a number that helped guide newcomers, including groups such as the Noob Community, DEFCON Academy, and the Lonely Hackers Club. Knowing this, I was fully assured that I would be welcomed and would fit in with the crowd. Afterwards, I started to research what I would like to see at the conference. With my realization of the number of people attending, there would be some extremely talented experts showing off cool things that everyone could appreciate. During my time in college, I would spend a lot of time with the engineering kids, collaborating on projects and gawking at the awesome work they had put together. Recalling this, I decided that anything robotics-related at the conference was a must-see. Looking into that, I found that there would be an entire group attending the conference working on and demo-ing the robots they designed and built. Writing this down, I started to think of other events that would be worthwhile to visit as a beginner. Firstly, I wanted to expose myself to various cybersecurity intro sessions and how to stand out in a competitive field. I found a number of talks based around starting a career in a time when AI is heavily used and how to refine my brand to stand out amongst the rest. Jotting some talks down that I would be interested in, I also looked into what was offered based around industrial cybersecurity and OT, as I had taken a very interesting class about that field in school and would love to hear more. With a collection of events I was planning on attending, all I had to do next was wait in anticipation for the day of the conference.

Finally, it was time for the conference. We had flown into Las Vegas the Sunday before the conference and spent the majority of the week doing some of the fun things the city had to offer. When Friday finally came, my father and I drove to the convention center and started with our schedule. When we first walked through the doors, it was difficult not to be intimidated. Massive crowds of people filled every space on the floor, with tides moving to see all that the villages had to offer. Gathering ourselves, we moved into the crowd and to our destination. Our first stop was the Noob Community. Here, I was hoping to talk to some professionals about how best to experience the conference and share my experience in the IT community. Additionally, we listened to a very interesting talk about how careers are faring in a world where AI dominates the market. Topics such as building your brand and demonstrating knowledge with projects were discussed here, including a suggestion to make a website and accompanying blog (an idea that may have led to the site and post you are reading now). After finishing at our first community and talk, I was hungry for more. I started searching the schedule for more ways to fill our day in between the highlighted events that I wanted to see. We were able to attend a number of talks regarding all sorts of topics, including game hacking, AI utilized in the government, and breaking the newest kinds of tech. The next event I wanted to attend was a resume review and workshop. I printed out a copy of my resume and was able to sit down with a professional who had a history of hiring people. He was able to give me valuable criticism about my resume and what direction I could take with it. This conversation was exactly what I had hoped I would receive by coming to this conference. With the help of a professional, I was excited and determined to take my next step in the field. The rest of the weekend held more panels and presentations, where I was able to hear more about all of the topics that interested me, including ICS/OT subjects and the ideas behind social engineering intrusions. We ended our trip with a final panel at the DEFCON Academy community, where experienced professionals, as well as those new in the field, discussed how to break into the world of cybersecurity and ways to get interested and involved. This final panel solidified my takeaways from the conference. There are so many different areas of this field that no one could explore them all. The goal is to find what most excites you and plunge yourself into that.

After this final presentation, it was time to pack our bags and head back home. The conference was a truly eye-opening experience and allowed me to network with tons of people from many different backgrounds all with the same goal, to actively grow in the field. With new ideas and strategies to help me develop my skills, I was excited for the next step. I feel very inspired, and I am looking forward to future DEFCONs where I can show off what I have learned.
