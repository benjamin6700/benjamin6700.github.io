October is finally upon us. This is one of my favorite times of year and a time when many gather to celebrate all it brings. As I’m writing this, I sit dead in the middle of fall, where the leaves change and the air chills. I, for one, am certainly settling into this haunting season. I’m enjoying my favorite October rituals, including watching my fair share of scary movies, my annual playthrough of Silent Hill 2, and my favorite, playing all the spooky board games I can get my hands on. So I figured there is no better way to kick off the season than with a review of a game I thoroughly enjoyed this season: Horrified: American Monsters.

One of many in the Horrified series, Horrified: American Monsters offers a look at a small town plagued by attacks from some of America’s most famous cryptids and legends. This scored major points for me in the style department. Firstly, I think the vibe of American cryptids is so untouched in the media. The essence of mystery and local legend surrounding these beings is such an exciting jumping-off point for stories. The fact that they don’t get the representation they deserve in pop culture is a little surprising. Something about a monster that could be lurking in your backyard or deep in the woods just does something for me. Additionally, the setting of a small, quiet town where all hell breaks loose has always been exciting for me. As played out as some might see it, I will always get pumped for some small-town chaos. That being said, to deserve a full score for concept, I would expect something completely new and unheard of. This leads me to give the concept a solid 4 out of 5.

![HAMMonsters](/assets/images/HAM-Monsters.jpeg){: .align-center width="600px"}

I sat down this weekend to play Horrified: American Monsters two separate times. The first time, I was accompanied by my partner in a two-player game. It had been some time since I played any Horrified game, so we took a second to read the setup, rules, and then jumped right in when we felt ready. The setup was relatively straightforward. There were some mix-ups with the monster setup that we didn’t notice until halfway through our game. The instructions for monster setup can be found in two places: one on the back of each monster mat, where the information about the monster is displayed, and the other in the back of the rule book. In order to set up and play the game properly, you have to read both. I feel like this could have all been placed in one central location to prevent confusion. This problem did not affect the game too much, so we let it slide and played on. We chose one of the monsters recommended for a novice game and one that we thought would be fun. After we had everything set up, we prepared for our battle with the Jersey Devil and the Banshee of the Badlands. Ultimately, we had a nice time playing together and strategizing on the best ways to save the town. One criticism my partner had was the way the instructions were worded for some of the monsters’ abilities. While I agree that some of them seemed a bit vague, I can also admit that I could have explained the rules a little better. With that in mind, I feel justified in letting this one slide. This and the earlier setup problems were my only complaints about clarity, however. That leads me to rank the game a 3 out of 5 for clarity. It’s nothing game-breaking, but these problems should have been found and fixed during playtesting. Moving forward, I do have a criticism that I will stand by regarding the rules for the Jersey Devil. The concept behind defeating the Jersey Devil is pretty unique and makes for some exciting gameplay. In order to destroy the monster, you must first find the cursed 13th child. By saving citizens in the town, you reveal clues alluding to who the 13th child might be. Some of the clues are things like wearing glasses or having a clock in the background. These clues line up with the pictures on the citizen cards that identify them. When my partner and I were playing, we were excited to finally reveal the identity of the 13th child. By the time we revealed the last clue, the three characteristics didn’t match up with any of the citizens. We checked the rules, where we found that the instructions had planned for this. Their solution was to grab another clue and disregard the first. This was a pretty big letdown, and something we both agreed could have been prevented with a little more effort from the design team when it came to the citizens. Even after this, I would still give design a 4 out of 5. Some of the mechanics could have been designed better, but the fact that you have six different monsters that all play differently is always a plus in my book.

![HAMSetup](/assets/images/HAM-Setup.jpeg){: .align-center width="600px"}

The next day, my partner and I invited two of our friends to come over and try the game out as well. In this four-player game, we decided to turn up the heat and try three monsters at once. We played with the Chupacabra, Bigfoot, and the Banshee of the Badlands for one last chaotic battle. Now that we’d had a few games under our belt, it was super easy for my partner and me to teach our friends, and we got the ball rolling pretty smoothly. We had a good time and managed to save the town just under the wire. The replayability of this game is great, allowing players to add up to four monsters in any combination. Since the replayability is so good and it isn’t difficult to grasp the basics, I would give the game-night-ability score a 5 out of 5.

![HAMCloseup](/assets/images/HAM-Closeup.jpeg){: .align-center width="600px"}

Overall, I really enjoyed this game. The minor hiccups with rules and design certainly did not take away from the fun I had with it. With such a super cool concept behind the game, it is easy to see why this series has been able to explore so many different types of fiction in its numerous sequels. I can see how easily these mechanics can be reused in other versions, and I am certainly interested in grabbing a few more to add to my collection. At the end of the day, I am happy to give Horrified: American Monsters an overall score of 4 out of 5. It wasn’t completely perfect, but it was a great game with great ideas.

The chilly fall air is upon us as the nights grow longer and the fear of the dark starts to creep in. I hope everyone is enjoying their horrific holiday just as much as I am. I will see you all again soon...
